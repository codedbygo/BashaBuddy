One morning in a Telangana village

The scholarship deadline is next week. Her daughter needs an income certificate.

She asks a neighbour which documents to take, loses a day's wages travelling to the service centre, and is told at the counter that one proof is missing. She comes back the next week. Or she pays an agent to find out what the website already says, in English.

Nothing here failed because she was ineligible.

It failed because the first question, "what do I need and where do I go?", had no good answer in her language.

Our insight

The hardest step is not the application. It is the question before it.

  • People already speak this question to neighbours, agents and counter staff, in Telugu or Hindi. Typing it into an English form is the unnatural part.
  • The answer is small and structured: which documents, who qualifies, which office, which steps. It does not need a chatbot that writes essays.
  • A wrong answer is worse than none. Invented eligibility rules cost wages and trust. So the assistant must answer only from sourced data and say "I don't know" otherwise.
  • Voice plus confirmation gives the speed of speaking with the accuracy of reading: the user sees and fixes the transcript before anything is answered.

The product

Speak. Confirm. Understand. Listen.

1

Choose a language. తెలుగు, हिंदी or English. The whole interface switches, not just the answers.

2

Tap and ask naturally. "రేషన్ కార్డు కోసం ఏ పత్రాలు కావాలి?" (What documents for a ration card?)

3

Confirm what was heard. Edit the transcript or record again. Nothing is answered unchecked.

4

Get the next step. Documents, eligibility or steps, with the official source. Tap to hear it read aloud.

  • Remembers context: after scholarships, "what documents?" answers for scholarships.
  • Asks when unsure: "How do I apply?" gets "Which service do you mean?"
  • No mic? No problem: typing and one-tap questions run the same flow.
  • Built for small Android screens: large targets, Indic fonts, works on a basic phone browser.

Trust by design

In public services, an honest "I don't know" beats a confident guess.

  • No language model writes the facts. Intent detection picks the service and question type; every sentence of substance comes from a structured, sourced dataset.
  • Every answer is labelled with its verification status and points to the official portal.
  • Unknown service? "I don't have verified information about that yet," instead of a plausible-sounding answer.
  • Never asks for OTPs, passwords or PINs, and warns users who try to share them: protection against the fraud that targets exactly these users.
  • Never claims to submit or track applications. It is not connected to any government database, and says so.
  • No accounts, no stored recordings, no personal data. Emergencies are pointed to 112.

Launch plan

One state, real data, real counters. Then scale what works.

Months 0 to 2

Verify

  • Top 20 services for Telangana and Andhra Pradesh checked against official portals
  • Source and date on every fact
  • Upgrade Telugu speech recognition
Months 2 to 4

Pilot

  • One college scholarship cell and one citizen service centre or NGO
  • Volunteers onboard first-time users
  • Weekly review of every "not helpful" answer
Months 4 to 9

Scale

  • More Indian languages and states
  • WhatsApp and phone access for basic phones
  • State department partnership to keep data current

Success metric: the share of users who reach the counter with every document on their first visit. Supporting signals: questions answered, "not helpful" rate, and repeat use.

Why voice, why now

The next hundred million Indians online will speak before they type.

  • Voice is the most natural interface for people who are new to the internet, less confident readers, or simply more comfortable in their mother tongue.
  • Open-source speech recognition now runs on a laptop, and every Android phone ships with speech built in. The cost of a voice-first public service has collapsed.
  • What is missing is a trustworthy layer between a spoken question and official information. That is BhashaBuddy.
